# Side Story Parfums — E‑commerce Audit
2 August 2026 · 35 pages reviewed at 1440/1920 (desktop) and 390/768 (mobile) · automated sweeps: metadata, structured data, internal links, contrast, tap targets, console errors, page weight

## Executive summary

The site's craft is unusually high for its stage: one generator produces every page, so typography, spacing and components are consistent in a way most stores never reach; images ship responsive in three formats; the a11y fundamentals (single h1, landmarks, alt text, focus management, reduced-motion) all pass. The two real problems were **honesty** and **discoverability**. The site was making claims that aren't true — fabricated press quotes, invented testimonials from named members of the public, five invented stockist relationships, a sample-credit offer, a free second sample, a Dedication service, a gifting page for a service the house doesn't offer. And it was invisible to machines: no canonicals, no Open Graph, no structured data, no sitemap, no robots.txt, plus a duplicate-content page. Both problems are now fixed in this commit; what remains is a content problem (FILLER needs real facts) and a roadmap problem (social proof, currency, retention — listed below).

## Implemented in this commit

**Critical — fabricated claims removed (trust/legal).**
The postbag testimonials attributed to "Marta L. · Lisbon", "Tom H. · Leeds", "Priya S. · Glasgow" are now FILLER lorem clearly marked. The press attributions (How To Spend It, Vogue Beauty) and the "Stocked at Liberty London" line are FILLER. The stockists page's five invented department-store relationships (Liberty, Le Bon Marché, Bergdorf Goodman, Lane Crawford, Grasse shop) are FILLER tiles. Real quotes and real stockists drop straight in.

**Critical — discontinued offers removed everywhere.**
The Dedication (bag drawer, bag page, checkout, account, confirmation, contact form topic) and the "Try a second story first — complimentary 2ml" checkbox (PDP and drawer) promised services the house doesn't offer. Gone. The last two surviving "sample cost redeemed" lines (drawer fine-print, PDP reassurance) now state the true £40 delivery threshold instead. `gifting.html` is no longer built — nothing had linked to it since the nav rename.

**Critical — checkout was charging wrong.** Found during audit: a duplicate of the entire PDP gallery/size-selector block in site.js meant every size tap ran twice. Removed (second such duplicate found this week — the first was the shelf controller).

**High — SEO baseline.** Every page: canonical (clean-URL form matching Vercel), og:title/description/image/url, twitter card. Homepage: Organization + WebSite (with SearchAction) JSON-LD. Every PDP: Product schema with brand, image and three GBP Offers. `sitemap.xml` (29 URLs) and `robots.txt` generated by the build from the pages actually written; bag/checkout/confirmation/account/404 excluded and disallowed. `story.html` — a byte-identical duplicate of a product story at a second URL — removed.

**High — mobile PDP conversion.** A sticky add-to-bag bar now appears on phones exactly when the real button scrolls off, in the thumb zone with safe-area padding, restating name, chosen size and price; it follows the size selector, adds to bag, and never renders on desktop.

**Medium — honesty markers.** The account page now says its orders are sample data (it presented fabricated order history as real). Checkout gained the reassurance line "Payment is handled by our payment processor — your card details never touch our servers", which is what the legal page already promises.

## Priority matrix — remaining

**Critical (content, not code — blocks launch)**
- Sibling Rivalry has no real data at all: story, summary, notes, style, stone, author — all FILLER.
- Returns window is FILLER in four places (PDP tab, shipping, terms). A store cannot trade in the UK without a stated returns policy.
- Seven author credits, perfumer credits, press quotes, stockists, postbag letters — all FILLER awaiting real facts.

**High**
- **Social proof**: no reviews anywhere. For £160 blind-bought fragrance this is the single largest conversion lever. Recommend a reviews platform (Okendo/Judge.me class) on PDPs + aggregate stars in Product schema — never hand-written quotes.
- **Currency**: AED appears in the ticker but the site sells only in GBP. Either a currency/market switcher or drop AED from the ticker. EU shipping threshold (£180) also reads as a different regime from UK £40 — confirm intended.
- **Express payment**: checkout is a demo form; when real, Apple Pay/Shop Pay/PayPal above the card form typically lifts mobile completion double digits. The PDP already shows an Apple Pay button — checkout should not be weaker than the PDP.
- **Scent-family filters** no longer match the supplied styles (e.g. Third Date "Green. Floral." vs the old Woods/Citrus/Powder/Spice/Amber families). Awaiting your family list — filters currently mislead.

**Medium**
- Internal links point at `.html` while Vercel serves clean URLs — every internal click risks a 308 hop. Worth a build flag that emits extension-less hrefs in production.
- "By nose" way-in card promises a two-question consultation that doesn't exist — either build the mini-quiz (high-affinity for this brand) or reword.
- Journal page exists with two entries and no cadence; either commit to it (SEO surface) or unlink until there's content.
- Wishlist / back-in-stock / bundles beyond the set: standard AOV/retention levers, all absent — fine for launch, plan for after.
- FAQ "Can I visit?" references a Grasse shop — verify or FILLER.

**Low**
- 404 could offer search, not just two links. Confirmation page could cross-sell the set to bottle buyers. Hero images could take `fetchpriority` hints per-page (LCP is already decent because of AVIF + preloaded fonts).

## What was deliberately *not* done

No urgency/scarcity theatre ("only 3 left") — wrong for this brand and this price point. No invented review counts or stars — schema ships without ratings until real ones exist. No layout redesigns for their own sake — the system is coherent; the audit found the weaknesses were claims and plumbing, not design.

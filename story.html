Sunday Service

Written by Morgan Childs · 7 min read · the story that became a fragrance

Home / Your Stories / Sunday Service

The road home has not been resurfaced in twenty years, and neither has the part of me that drives it. Past the reservoir the trees close over the lane the way they always did, and there — still standing, still rusted the colour of a wet penny — is the gate we used to climb.

I am late for my niece’s christening. I park badly, as everyone does here, and cross the gravel with my collar wrong. Inside, the cool oaky air arrives before the organ does — polish and cold stone and the ghost of last week’s lilies — and I am eleven again in an itching jumper, and thirty-eight, and neither.

My mother is three rows from the front, in the coat. She turns before I have made a sound, which she has done my whole life and has never once explained.

The music was the same. The cool, oaky air of the church was the same. Only the child being held over the font was new, and she was furious about it, and everybody laughed, and for a moment the building held all of us at once — the ones who came back, the ones who never left, and the ones who were only ever here in the smell of the place.

Afterwards there are sandwiches in the hall and someone’s husband has opinions about the bypass. I stand by the door with a paper cup and let the incense come off my coat in the cold.
